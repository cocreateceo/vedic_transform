# Movement Everyday (Vyayama) — Improvement Dossier

## 1. Snapshot

**Tagline:** "The body was built to move daily. Vyayama is not exercise — it is the maintenance ritual of being human."

**What the pillar offers today:**
- **Core principle:** the body must be challenged daily but not destroyed; Ayurveda's distinction between *vyayama* (rejuvenating exercise) and *ati-vyayama* (depleting overexercise). "Consistent input over maximum output." "30 min/day every day beats 90 min three times a week."
- **Priority order:** walking → yoga → mobility → strength.
- **Mood claim:** "A 20-minute walk produces measurably more antidepressant effect than most prescriptions, with effect onset in under an hour."
- **Mechanism claim:** daily (esp. outdoor) walking produces BDNF, "the molecule most strongly correlated with new neuron growth and resistance to depression," with the effect requiring "only 7,000–10,000 steps daily."
- **Daily practice:** 5 min joint rotation → 10–15 min yoga (Surya Namaskar) → 20–30 min outdoor walk → strength 2×/week → one long 60–90 min session/week → never train hard on <6 h sleep → close with 2 min Savasana.
- **Scripture:** Hatha Yoga Pradipika 1.1 and 1.65 (Svatmarama); Bhagavad Gita 6.17.
- **Obstacles:** no time; recurrent injury (= ati-vyayama, "drop intensity by half"); boredom.
- **Dosha close:** Vata = gentle/rhythmic/grounding; Pitta = cooling/non-competitive; Kapha = vigorous/sweat-inducing.

### Ayurvedic vyayama / ati-vyayama / "half capacity" — the missing real references

The app's central Ayurvedic claims (vyayama vs ati-vyayama; "drop intensity by half") are **legitimate and traceable to primary texts** — they are just uncited. Adding them is the single highest-authority upgrade available:

- **"Exercise to half of one's strength" (balardha / ardha-shakti):** ✅ **Ashtanga Hridaya, Sutrasthana 2.10–11** (Vagbhata): *"ardhaśaktyā niṣevyastu balibhiḥ snigdhabhojibhiḥ"* — the strong and the well-nourished should exercise using only **half** of their capacity (and only in the cool seasons; lightly otherwise). [5][6] Parallel: **Charaka Samhita, Sutrasthana 7.31** (vyayama benefits) and the "stop before exhaustion / ardha-shakti" guidance at **Su. 7.33–34**. [7]
- **The objective endpoint marker:** sweating of the **forehead, axillae (armpits), nose and joints, plus dryness of the mouth**, is the classical sign that one has reached half-strength — i.e. *stop here*. (Sushruta Samhita, Chikitsasthana 24.47; echoed in Charaka Su. 7.) [7] This is a concrete, measurable cue the app should surface to users.
- **Benefits of correct vyayama:** *laghava* (lightness), *karma-sāmarthya* (work capacity), *sthairya* (stability), *duḥkha-sahiṣṇutā* (tolerance to discomfort), *doṣa-kṣaya/-balance*, and *agni-vṛddhi* (improved digestion). — **Charaka Su. 7.32.** [7]
- **Ati-vyayama (the warning):** ✅ **Ashtanga Hridaya, Sutrasthana 2.13–14** (Vagbhata): excess exercise causes *kāsa* (cough), *jvara* (fever), *chardi* (vomiting), thirst, emaciation, dyspnoea, fainting, bleeding disorders, and fatigue — "one who over-exerts perishes **like a lion that drags down an elephant**" (*gajaṃ siṃha ivākarṣan… vinaśyati*). [5][6] This is a vivid, real, quotable line that the app's "I keep getting injured" obstacle is implicitly invoking — it should quote it.

### Science claims

| Claim (current text) | Verdict | Correct picture + source |
|---|---|---|
| **"A 20-minute walk produces measurably more antidepressant effect than most prescriptions, with effect onset in under an hour."** | ❌ **WRONG / overstated — fix this.** | Two errors. (1) **"More than most prescriptions"** is not supported. The best evidence (Noetel et al., *BMJ* 2024 network meta-analysis, 218 RCTs / 14,170 participants) found walking/jogging effective for depression (Hedges' g ≈ **−0.62** vs controls), **but the much-publicised "exercise beats medication by ~1.5×" framing was explicitly debunked** — it arose from comparing effect sizes across *very-low-quality* prior reviews, not from a head-to-head. Noetel rated confidence as **LOW** for walking/jogging and very low for other modalities, and found exercise **complements/combines with** SSRIs rather than beating them. [8][9][10] (2) **"Effect onset in under an hour"** conflates the *acute mood lift* from a single bout (real, but transient) with *antidepressant* effect (which accrues over weeks). **Recommended rewrite:** "A 20-minute walk reliably lifts mood within the hour; sustained over weeks, walking/jogging is an evidence-based treatment for depression — comparable in effect to (and best combined with) standard care." |
| **"Daily walking produces BDNF, the molecule most strongly correlated with new neuron growth and resistance to depression."** | ⚠️ **Directionally true, overstated superlative.** | Aerobic exercise (including walking) reliably raises circulating BDNF — meta-analyses show large effects across multiple populations, and a 2025 meta-analysis on walking/running/cycling in older adults confirms aerobic exercise increases BDNF. [11][12] BUT "**the** molecule most strongly correlated" is an unverifiable superlative — BDNF is *a* key neurotrophin (Ratey's "Miracle-Gro for the brain"), not provably "the most" of anything. Soften to "one of the key molecules" and drop the absolute. |
| **"The effect requires only 7,000–10,000 steps daily."** | ✅ **Verified (and now better-sourced).** | The 2025 *Lancet Public Health* dose-response meta-analysis (Ding et al.) found inflection points around **5,000–7,000 steps/day** for all-cause mortality, CVD, dementia and falls; **7,000 steps/day ≈ 47% lower all-cause mortality vs 2,000 (HR 0.53)**; 10,000 remains a valid stretch target. [13] Tighten the app copy to "**~7,000 steps/day captures most of the benefit; 10,000 is a stretch goal, not a requirement**" — this is more accurate and more encouraging than implying 10k is needed. |
| **"30 min/day every day beats 90 min 3×/week."** | ⚠️ **Defensible framing, not a hard finding.** | Consistency and reduced sedentary time have independent benefits, and frequency aids habit formation; but total volume/intensity also matter (WHO: 150–300 min/week moderate *or* 75–150 vigorous). Keep as a motivational heuristic, not a quantitative claim. |
| **Surya Namaskar as the "universal sequence."** | ✅ **Reasonable.** | Studies show Surya Namaskar improves flexibility, muscular endurance, pulmonary function and HRV (parasympathetic shift); dynamic/fast SN reaches aerobic-comparable intensity, slow SN matches yoga-level loads. Note one caveat to honour internally: a single SN session is *low-to-moderate* intensity, so it complements but does not replace the walk/strength components. [14][15] |

### Proposed additions (raise authority)

1. **Cite the Ayurvedic primaries** for every Ayurvedic assertion (AH Su. 2.10–14; Charaka Su. 7.31–34; Sushruta Ci. 24.47) — see §2 above. This converts asserted folk-Ayurveda into verifiable scholarship.
2. **Add a fourth scripture slot** with the lion-and-elephant ati-vyayama line (AH Su. 2.14) — it is more on-domain than HYP 1.1's generic "salutation," and dramatizes the pillar's core warning.
3. **Replace the antidepressant claim** with the Noetel/BMJ-grounded version and cite it.
4. **Add a "longevity markers" frame**: VO₂max and grip/leg strength are among the strongest predictors of all-cause mortality; the Sitting-Rising Test (Brito 2014: composite <8 ≈ 2× mortality over 6.3 yr) is a free, in-app-testable marker. [16][17]

## 3. Competitive Analysis

**1. *Spark* — John Ratey (2008).** The popular-science anchor for the exercise→brain story; coined "BDNF = Miracle-Gro for the brain" and argues exercise rivals medication for mild-to-moderate depression, ADHD and anxiety. *Does well:* makes the neuroscience visceral and motivating. *We can borrow:* the BDNF narrative (but use Ratey's actual, more careful claim — "as effective as," not "more than"). [18]

**2. *Built to Move* — Kelly & Juliet Starrett (2023).** Mobility/longevity manual: **10 vital-sign tests + 10 practices** (Sit-and-Rise, shoulder rotation, breath-hold, Old-Man balance, sitting on the floor, 800 m walk, etc.). *Does well:* turns "movement" into **self-administered diagnostics with pass/fail thresholds** — exactly the measurability our pillar lacks. *They cover, we don't:* baseline mobility tests, "movement snacks," sitting-on-the-floor time, daily step floor. [19][20]

**3. *Outlive* — Peter Attia (2023).** Frames training around the **"Centenarian Decathlon"** and **four pillars: Zone 2 (aerobic base), VO₂max, strength, stability.** *Does well:* backward-design from "what do you want to do at 90?"; concrete targets (e.g., maintain VO₂max >30 ml/kg/min). *They cover, we don't:* structured progression across distinct energy systems, stability/balance training, longevity targets. [21]

**4. Hatha Yoga Pradipika / Gheranda Samhita (primary texts).** The traditional spine of the asana lineage. *Does well:* roots asana in *steadiness for meditation*, not fitness — which our pillar already nails. *We can deepen:* name specific foundational asanas the texts prescribe (HYP lists e.g. Svastikasana, Gomukhasana, Padmasana; Gheranda Samhita's 32 asanas) rather than leaving "yoga" generic.

**5. Apps — Down Dog, Apple Fitness+, Peloton, Strava, Alo Moves.**
- **Down Dog:** AI-generated, never-repeating yoga sequences with granular customization (level, pace, focus, voice). *We lack:* any generated/guided sequence.
- **Apple Fitness+:** polished video library + deep Apple Watch metric integration, advanced yoga/strength tracks, Strava sync (2025). *We lack:* guided audio/video, wearable metrics.
- **Peloton:** studio-class energy, instructor charisma, leaderboard/community. *We lack:* social/accountability layer.
- **Strava:** the activity-logging + segment/social standard; not guided. *We lack:* GPS walk logging and streaks.
- **Alo Moves:** 4,000+ curated classes from 70+ instructors across styles. *We lack:* depth of guided content.

## 4. App Feature / UX Recommendations

1. **Surya Namaskar guided walkthrough** (timer + 12-pose illustrated/animated sequence, breath cues, slow vs dynamic pace toggle). Highest-impact missing artifact for a "movement" pillar.
2. **Movement vital-signs panel** (borrow Built to Move): in-app **Sitting-Rising Test** (score 0–10), shoulder-rotation reach, single-leg balance timer, 30-second sit-to-stand. Store baseline; re-test at Day 1 / 24 / 48 to show progression. Each test links to its evidence (Brito 2014 for SRT).
3. **Half-strength intensity governor (signature feature).** Operationalize the Ayurvedic *ardha-shakti* rule: a simple post-session check ("Mouth dry? Light sweat on forehead/armpits? Could you have done much more?") that classifies the session as *under / vyayama / ati-vyayama* and nudges intensity. Optional HR-zone integration if a wearable is connected (cap daily hard effort).
4. **Step + walk logging with streaks**, target line at **7,000 steps** (not 10k) with 10k as a "stretch" badge. Pull from HealthKit / Google Fit if available; manual otherwise.
5. **"Movement snacks" / sedentary-break nudges** — micro-prompts to stand/walk after long sitting (addresses the "no time" obstacle directly).
6. **Sleep-aware deload** — wire the "never train hard on <6 h sleep" rule to the Sleep pillar so the app auto-suggests "walk-only today" after a poor night.
7. **Mood check-in around the walk** (1-tap before/after) to let users *see* the acute mood lift — turns the (now-corrected) antidepressant claim into personal evidence rather than a contested headline.

## 5. Practice & Program Design

### Sharper daily protocol (with the half-strength cap made explicit)
- **Mobilize (5 min):** joint rotations head-to-ankle (current text is good).
- **Yoga (10–15 min):** guided Surya Namaskar — slow rounds for steadiness, build to dynamic rounds for aerobic load.
- **Walk (20–30 min):** outdoor, morning light, pre-breakfast preferred; target contributing toward ~7,000 steps/day.
- **Strength 2×/week (15–20 min):** bodyweight progression (squat → split squat; push-up progressions; hinge; carry). Add **stability** work (single-leg balance, dead-bug) per Attia's fourth pillar — currently absent.
- **One long session/week (60–90 min):** hike/swim/dance/sport.
- **Always close** with 2 min Savasana.
- **Stop rule (new, explicit):** end any hard effort at the *ardha-shakti* signs — light sweat + dry mouth + "could do more." Pushing past = ati-vyayama.

### Dosha personalization (extends current close)
| Dosha | Emphasis | Add (new specifics) | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Vata** | Gentle, rhythmic, grounding: slow yoga, swimming, walking | Warm up longer; consistent *timing*; strength with control over speed | Running on hard surfaces; erratic/over-long sessions (depletes Vata) |
| **Pitta** | Cooling, non-competitive: swimming, hiking, gentle/yin yoga | Train in cooler hours; remove leaderboards/PR-chasing for this type | Midday heat; competitive intensity; ego-driven loading |
| **Kapha** | Vigorous, sweat-inducing: running, HIIT, dance, faster Surya Namaskar | Higher frequency; morning activation to break stagnation | Skipping/under-dosing; long passive rest |

### 48-day progression milestones
- **Day 1 (baseline):** record SRT score, balance time, resting steps, current weekly minutes, baseline mood.
- **Days 1–12 (Establish):** non-negotiable daily walk + 5 daily Surya Namaskar rounds; goal = *streak*, not intensity. Introduce half-strength rule.
- **Days 13–24 (Build):** add strength 2×/week + stability drills; raise to 10–12 SN rounds; first re-test at Day 24.
- **Days 25–36 (Integrate):** add weekly long session; refine dosha-specific modality; aim 7,000 steps most days.
- **Days 37–48 (Consolidate):** full protocol habitual; **Day 48 re-test** SRT/balance/steps/mood and show deltas.

### Measurable markers of progress
- Sitting-Rising Test score (target ≥8, ideally 10). [16]
- Single-leg balance time; shoulder-rotation reach (Built to Move tests). [19]
- Daily step average trend (toward 7,000). [13]
- Resting heart rate / HRV if a wearable is connected.
- Walk-streak length and self-reported pre/post-walk mood delta.
- Subjective recovery: how often sessions cross into ati-vyayama (should trend down).
