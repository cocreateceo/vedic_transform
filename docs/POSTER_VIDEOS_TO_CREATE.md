# Poster Videos — One Video Per Poster (Handoff Brief)

> **Self-contained.** Hand this whole file to your video tool (CapCut
> Script-to-Video / Pictory / InVideo) or to another agent/terminal.
> Each of the **31 teaching posters** becomes its own video; the script is
> derived directly from that poster's content (title → hook, scripture →
> authority quote, sections → beats, tagline → close).

## How these map

Each poster *is* the storyboard. Two ways to produce the visual:
- **Simplest:** use the poster image itself on screen and slowly pan/zoom
  across its sections (Ken Burns) as the narration walks through them, or
- **Richer:** generate matching b-roll per beat (Veo / Pippit / stock).

### P1 — 5-Step Morning Routine for Manifestation
**Pillar:** morning-initiation · **Length:** ~2.5 min · **Poster:** `morning-routine-5-step`

```
Welcome, dear friend. Today I share with you a five-step morning routine for manifestation — a way to begin each day with intention and grace.

The sages of old gave us a timeless truth. In Yoga Sutra one point two, Patanjali says: Yogah chitta vritti nirodhah — when the mind becomes still, inner clarity awakens. Let this stillness be the foundation of your morning.

Step one: wake up early. Rise calmly and consciously, around five in the morning, or simply twenty minutes earlier than usual. Drink a glass of warm water. Avoid the phone for the first twenty to thirty minutes. Begin the day with silence, not distraction. This sets a peaceful tone, boosts your energy, sharpens your focus, and strengthens your willpower and discipline.

Step two: center your breath. Sit upright. Inhale slowly for four seconds, deep and full. Exhale gently for six seconds, steady and soft. For two to five minutes, calm the nervous system. This reduces stress, improves the flow of energy, deepens concentration, and brings balance to body and mind.

Step three: connect to awareness, to the Source. Sit quietly in stillness for a minute or two. Bring your attention to the present moment. Become gently aware of your inner state, and feel your connection to the universal energy — to Brahman. This deepens self-awareness, creates inner peace, and aligns you with higher wisdom.

Step four: gratitude and thought alignment. Mentally list three things you are grateful for. Set one positive intention for the day. And whenever a negative thought arises, replace it gently with a constructive one. This rewires the mind for positivity, builds emotional resilience, and attracts positive experiences.

Step five: manifestation and energy alignment. For two minutes, visualize your key goal for the next forty-eight days. See yourself already succeeding. Invite clarity, strength, and divine guidance. Hold that image for a few quiet breaths. This clarifies your purpose, boosts your confidence, and aligns your energy with your goals.

This practice is inspired by the wisdom of the Patanjali Yoga Sutras.

Begin tomorrow. Begin gently. Namaste.
```

### P12 — Pranayama for Vata Dosha
**Pillar:** breathing-meditation · **Length:** ~2.5 min · **Poster:** `vata-pranayama`

```
Friends, let us learn the pranayama for the Vata dosha. Vata is the union of air and space — light, dry, cold, mobile, and subtle. When it falls out of balance, it brings anxiety, restlessness, insomnia, dry skin, and irregular energy. So the aim of our breathing is to calm the nervous system, to create warmth and inner grounding, to support digestion, and to invite restful sleep.

The first practice is Nadi Shodhana, the alternate nostril breathing. Sit comfortably with a straight spine. Close the right nostril and inhale slowly through the left. Close both for a moment, then exhale through the right. Inhale right, close both, exhale left — that is one round. Practise five to ten minutes. This balances the left and right channels of energy, calms the nervous system, and quiets overthinking. Best practised in early morning or evening.

The second practice is Ujjayi, the victorious breath. Sit comfortably. Inhale slowly through the nose with the throat gently narrowed, as if whispering the sound "ha" softly. Exhale through the nose with the same gentle sound. Keep the breath smooth, long, and even for five to ten minutes. This produces inner warmth, gathers scattered energy, and helps with insomnia and anxiety. Practise it before meditation or sleep.

The third practice is Deep Belly Breathing, Dirgha Shvasana. Sit or lie down comfortably, one hand on the chest and one on the belly. Inhale slowly through the nose, expanding the belly. Exhale slowly, softening the belly. Keep the breath deep, slow, and natural for five to ten minutes. This calms the mind instantly, relaxes the nervous system, and supports digestion and elimination.

To anchor these practices: sit in a warm, quiet place, with support if you need it. Keep the breath slow and gentle — never forceful or fast. Eat warm, nourishing, cooked foods, and hydrate with warm water or herbal tea. Lengthen the exhale to calm Vata, and pair your pranayama with breath-awareness or mantra meditation.

For Vata, the breath is the anchor. When the breath becomes slow and steady, life becomes peaceful and balanced. Namaste.
```

### P13 — Pranayama for Kapha Dosha
**Pillar:** breathing-meditation · **Length:** ~2.5 min · **Poster:** `kapha-pranayama`

```
Friends, let us learn the pranayama for the Kapha dosha. Kapha is the union of earth and water — heavy, slow, cold, stable, and dense. When it falls out of balance, it brings lethargy, low motivation, excess sleep, weight gain, and congestion. So the aim of our breathing is to increase energy and motivation, to stimulate the metabolism, to clear congestion, and to bring lightness and joy.

The first practice is Kapalabhati, the skull-shining breath. Sit comfortably with a straight spine. Inhale normally, then exhale forcefully through the nose by drawing in the abdomen — the inhalation comes by itself, passive and easy. Begin with twenty to thirty strokes and gradually build to sixty or more. Rest, then repeat three rounds. This increases energy and alertness, clears mucus and congestion, and removes laziness and heaviness. Best practised in early morning on an empty stomach.

The second practice is Bhastrika, the bellows breath. Sit in a comfortable posture. Inhale deeply and exhale forcefully through both nostrils — equal, powerful, and rhythmic. Ten breaths make one round. Start with three rounds, resting between them. This generates heat and vitality, improves circulation, strengthens the lungs, and boosts immunity and willpower.

The third practice is Surya Bhedana, the right nostril breathing. Close the left nostril with the ring finger and inhale slowly through the right. Close the right with the thumb and exhale through the left. Inhale right, exhale left — that is one round. Practise five to ten minutes. This kindles internal heat, activates digestion and metabolism, and removes cold, heaviness, and dullness.

To support this practice: breathe on an empty stomach in a well-ventilated place, keeping the spine straight and the body alert. Stay active — exercise, walk, and practise Surya Namaskar daily. Eat light, warm, dry, and spicy foods, and avoid sweets and cold drinks. Drink warm water and herbal teas of ginger, tulsi, and cinnamon. Wake early, avoid daytime sleep, and seek sunlight and fresh air.

For Kapha, the breath is the fire that awakens. Strong, conscious breath makes Kapha light, active, and balanced. Namaste.
```
